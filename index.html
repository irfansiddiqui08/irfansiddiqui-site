  • Business contact details: the name and job title of an owner or director, and a business email address.

  • What we say to each other by email.

Where it comes from

Public sources: business websites, Google Maps listings, Companies House and trade directories. I use an email-verification service to check that an address works before I write to it.

Why I use it, and the legal basis

I use it to offer website and SEO services to businesses that could clearly benefit. My legal basis is legitimate interests (UK GDPR / EU GDPR Article 6(1)(f)). I only send unsolicited marketing email where the law allows it. In the UK, that means limited companies and LLPs. I don't cold-email sole traders or partnerships, and I don't use email tracking pixels.

How long I keep it

If you don't reply, I delete your details within 12 months of first contact. If you become a client, I keep records for as long as the law requires for tax and accounting.

Opting out

Reply "no thanks" or "unsubscribe" to any email. I'll stop contacting you and keep only the minimum needed to make sure you aren't contacted again: your email address or domain, the date, and the reason.

Who else handles it

My email provider (GoDaddy Professional Email) and my email-verification service, only to provide their services to me. I don't sell or share your data with anyone else. I work from India, so your data is handled outside the UK and EU.

Your rights

You can ask to see, correct or delete your data, or object to me using it, by emailing me. If you're unhappy with my response, you can complain to the UK Information Commissioner's Office (ico.org.uk) or your local data-protection authority.
